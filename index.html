Research Focus

➤ Visible-Shortwave Infrared Imaging Spectroscopy

As we enter an era of global imaging spectroscopy, we need methods that are scalable, transferable, and applicable to a wide range of ecosystems. I believe that the key is to meeting this challenge is to develop methods that are grounded in the underlying physiological and ecological processes, that leverage our knowledge of environmental and biological optics, and maintain integratability with existing ecological models. Fortunately, physically grounded remote sensing is a whole lot of fun!

[image]

➤ Plant Water Relations & Tree Drought

Which trees are most vulnerable to drought, and how can we predict when they will die? The location and extent of tree mortality during and after drought events is hard to predict, and often comes as what Prof. Anna Trugman and co. call an "ecological surprise". I integrate remote sensing technologies with a physiological understanding of plant water use to better understand which trees are most vulnerable to drought.

[image]

➤ Leaf to Canopy Spectral Scaling

My white whale! We have a strong, mechanistic understanding of plant organismal processes, how they intersect with physiology, and even (to a certain extent) how they affect leaf spectral reflectance. But scaling this knowledge to the canopy level and using it to interpret remotely sensed data is nontrivial. In all my research, I keep an eye on how leaf-level spectral relationships scale (or fail to scale!) to the canopy level.
