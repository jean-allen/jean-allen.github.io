How do leaf spectra change as leaves dry?

This tool lets you see the data behind Allen et al. (2026), New Phytologist. We collected measurements of leaf water potential, leaf water per unit area, and leaf spectral reflectance for 13 tree and shrub species as they slowly dried out on a benchtop, alongside concurrent. Pick a species and a leaf, then drag the slider (or hit play) to watch its spectrum change as it dries. You can also follow along with the pressure-volume curve and the EWT values using the plots below.

It's a little hard to see the underlying trends in the raw spectra, so here I've modeled the reflectance at each wavelength as a quadratic function of leaf water per area. (Each sample has its own unique fit, so the spectral changes visualized here are still specific to each leaf.) This makes it easier to see the directional trends, which I think is what's important here, but if you want to see the raw data in all its messiness, you can download it on EcoSIS, or on the GitHub repo for this project (which also includes all code used in the analysis). Cheers!
